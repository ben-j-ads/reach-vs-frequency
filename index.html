How it works

The model

Impressions = budget / CPM x 1,000. Average frequency f is impressions divided by audience. How many impressions each person gets follows a negative binomial distribution with mean f and a shape parameter that sets how uneven delivery is. A very large shape is the Poisson, the even case.

P(0) = (shape / (shape + f))^shape P(k+1) = P(k) x (shape + k) / (k + 1) x f / (shape + f) reach n+ = 1 - sum of P(k) for k below n past enough = (f - E[min(k, n)]) / f
The verdict

over-frequency: more than 40% of impressions land past your "enough" point.

spread thin: fewer than half of your audience reaches your "enough" point and under 20% of impressions are past it.

balanced: neither. The 40% and 20% cut-offs are this tool's choice, not a finding.

Read this before you trust the numbers

This is a model, not your delivery data. The platform's auction, caps and learning phase change who sees what. Use it to compare plans and see the direction, and check reach and frequency in the platform's own report.

Reach is people, not accounts. Duplicate profiles, shared devices and logged-out views blur it.

More frequency is not always waste. Message fatigue is real, but so is the need for enough exposures to be noticed. Past "enough" is your own threshold, not a law.
